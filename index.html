About the Nonnegative Inverse Eigenvalue Problem

The mathematical framework governing entrywise nonnegative realizations, structured subproblems, and universal spectral invariants.

01

The NIEP Formulation

Given a multiset of $n$ complex numbers $\sigma = \{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_n\}$, does there exist an entrywise nonnegative matrix $A \ge 0$ of order $n$ whose spectrum equals $\sigma$?

$$\begin{aligned} A &= (a_{ij}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}, \quad a_{ij} \ge 0 \\[4pt] \sigma(A) &= \{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_n\}, \quad \det(\lambda I - A) = \prod_{i=1}^n (\lambda - \lambda_i) \end{aligned}$$

Despite over a century of study since the roots of Perron–Frobenius theory, a general constructive characterization for arbitrary dimension $n \ge 5$ remains one of the premier unsolved problems in spectral matrix theory.

02

Subproblems & Variants

Several foundational variants isolate specific algebraic, geometric, and topological structures:

Symmetric NIEP (SNIEP)

Requires $A = A^T$. Restricts all eigenvalues to $\mathbb{R}$ with real orthogonal eigenvectors.

Real NIEP (RNIEP)

Requires $A \ge 0$ with $\sigma \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ (not necessarily symmetric; strictly broader than SNIEP for $n \ge 5$).

Stochastic NIEP (S-NIEP)

Requires row sums equal to $1$, normalizing spectral radius $\rho(A) = 1$ and connecting directly to Karpelevič regions.

Spectral Padding

Realization by an $N \times N$ matrix with $N \ge n$, padding with $N - n$ auxiliary zero eigenvalues.

03

Necessary Conditions

Any realizable spectrum $\sigma = \{\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n\}$ must satisfy universal spectral and trace invariants:

1

Conjugate Symmetry

$$\sigma = \bar{\sigma}$$

Spectrum must be closed under complex conjugation because $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$.

2

Perron Root Dominance

$$\rho = \max_{1 \le i \le n} |\lambda_i| \in \sigma$$

The spectral radius must be an eigenvalue of $A$ (Perron–Frobenius theorem).

3

Power Sum Traces

$$s_k = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i^k = \operatorname{tr}(A^k) \ge 0 \quad (\forall k \ge 1)$$

Since $A \ge 0$, all powers $A^k \ge 0$, so their diagonal entries and traces must be nonnegative.

4

Loewy–London Inequalities

$$s_k^m \le n^{m-1} s_{km} \quad (\forall k, m \ge 1)$$

Higher-order moments derived from entrywise Cauchy–Schwarz and Hölder inequalities.

Open Problems & Active Conjectures

Crucial unanswered questions, structural conjectures, and geometric frontiers shaping modern research in spectral nonnegativity.

01

General NIEP Solvability for $n \ge 5$

$$\sigma \in \operatorname{NIEP}(n) \iff (\sigma = \bar{\sigma}) \;\wedge\; \Phi_n(\sigma) \ge 0 \quad (n \ge 5)$$

While completely solved for $n \le 4$, dimensions $n \ge 5$ remain open. Because moment trace conditions are known to be insufficient, identifying an explicit, minimal system of polynomial inequalities or geometric criteria that completely characterizes realizability is one of the premier challenges in combinatorial matrix theory.

Key Challenge: Determine the exact semi-algebraic boundary equations distinguishing realizable spectra from trace-valid but unrealizable multisets.
02

Minimal Nonzero Spectrum Padding

$$N_{\min}(\sigma) = \min \{N \ge n : \sigma \cup \{0\}^{N-n} \in \operatorname{NIEP}(N)\}$$

By the Boyle–Handelman theorem (1991), any conjugation-closed nonzero spectrum with a strictly dominant positive Perron root ($\lambda_1 > |\lambda_j|$) and $s_m \ge 0$ with $s_m > 0 \Rightarrow s_{km} > 0$ ($\forall k \ge 1$) is the nonzero spectrum of a primitive nonnegative matrix of some higher order $N \ge n$. Determining sharp general upper bounds on the minimal embedding dimension $N_{\min}(\sigma)$ remains open.

Key Challenge: Establish polynomial or dimensional upper bounds on $N_{\min}$ as a function of the spectral gap $\rho - \max_{i \ne 1} \operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i)$.
03

Geometry of Preserving Cones $\mathcal{P}_n$

$$\mathcal{P}_n = \{p \in \mathbb{R}[x] : p(A) \ge 0 \quad \forall A \ge 0\}$$

The cone $\mathcal{P}_n$ of polynomials preserving nonnegative matrices provides infinite families of necessary trace invariants. While recently proven to be non-polyhedral for degree $d \ge 2n$ with arbitrarily negative coefficients, characterizing its extreme rays, boundary equations, and spherical measure remains active research.

Key Challenge: Find exact boundary algebraic representations for $\mathcal{P}_n$ when restricted to degrees $d \ge 2n$.
04

The RNIEP vs. SNIEP Boundary Gap

$$\mathcal{G}_n = \operatorname{RNIEP}(n) \setminus \operatorname{SNIEP}(n) \neq \emptyset \quad (n \ge 5)$$

In 1996, Johnson, Laffey, and Loewy proved that $\operatorname{SNIEP}(n) \subsetneq \operatorname{RNIEP}(n)$ for all $n \ge 5$. Realizing spectra non-symmetrically unlocks non-orthogonal eigenvectors that accommodate real spectra where symmetric matrices fail. Exactly delineating the geometric gap and boundary between these two semi-algebraic sets remains unresolved.

Key Challenge: Identify spectral criteria that definitively dictate when a real spectrum demands a non-symmetric realization.

Landmark Results & Milestones

Chronological developments establishing the boundaries, characterizations, and solvability of the NIEP.

1907 · 1912

Perron–Frobenius Spectral Theory

O. Perron · G. Frobenius

Mathematische Annalen, 64(2): 248–263 · Sitzungsber. Preuss. Akad. Wiss. Berlin: 456–477

Established that any nonnegative matrix $A \ge 0$ has a real, maximal eigenvalue $\rho(A) \ge |\lambda|$ with an associated nonnegative eigenvector. For irreducible matrices, $\rho(A)$ is algebraically simple, strictly positive, and any peripheral eigenvalues on the circle $|z| = \rho(A)$ are distributed with exact cyclic root-of-unity symmetry.

DOI Theory Wiki
1949

Suleĭmanova's Theorem

H. R. Suleĭmanova

Doklady Akademii Nauk SSSR, 66(3): 343–345

Proved the earliest major sufficiency result for real spectra: if $\sigma$ contains a single positive eigenvalue $\lambda_0 > 0$ and all other $\lambda_i \le 0$, then $\sigma$ is realizable by a nonnegative matrix if and only if the trace condition $\sum_i \lambda_i \ge 0$ holds. This became the foundation for companion matrix constructions and perturbed realizations.

Suleĭmanova Wiki
1951

Karpelevič's Boundary Characterization

F. I. Karpelevič

Izvestiya Akademii Nauk SSSR. Seriya Matematicheskaya, 15(4): 361–383

Completely determined the compact region $\mathcal{K}_n \subset \mathbb{C}$ of all possible eigenvalues of $n \times n$ stochastic matrices. The boundary consists of curvilinear arcs connecting roots of unity corresponding to Farey fractions $p/q \le 1$ with $q \le n$. In 1997, H. Ito reformulated these boundary curves as explicit minimal algebraic polynomial equations.

DOI Karpelevič Wiki
1978 · 1979

Loewy–London Inequalities & Order 3 Resolution

R. Loewy · D. London

Linear and Multilinear Algebra, 6(1): 83–90

Introduced higher trace inequalities $s_k^m \le n^{m-1} s_{km}$, ruling out infinite candidate spectra that satisfy ordinary trace positivity. They posed the fundamental problem of characterizing the cone $\mathcal{P}_n$ of polynomials preserving nonnegative matrices, completely solving the NIEP for $n = 3$ and trace-zero $n = 4$.

DOI NIEP Wiki
1979 · 1981

General JLL Power-Sum Inequalities

C. R. Johnson

Linear and Multilinear Algebra, 10(2): 113–130

Independently established and generalized the Johnson–Loewy–London (JLL) inequalities $s_k^m \le n^{m-1} s_{km}$ for all nonnegative matrices and integers $k, m \ge 1$, providing fundamental necessary conditions on higher spectral moments across all matrix orders.

DOI NIEP Wiki
1991

Boyle–Handelman Spectral Realization Theorem

M. Boyle · D. Handelman

Annals of Mathematics, 133(2): 249–316

Resolved the nonzero spectrum problem for primitive real nonnegative matrices via symbolic dynamics: a conjugation-closed nonzero list $\sigma$ is the nonzero spectrum of a primitive $A \in \mathcal{M}_N(\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0})$ for some $N \ge |\sigma|$ if and only if it has a strictly dominant positive Perron eigenvalue $\lambda_1 > |\lambda_j|$ and satisfies $s_m \ge 0$ together with $s_m > 0 \Rightarrow s_{km} > 0$ for all $k \ge 1$.

DOI Boyle–Handelman
1998 · 2007

Full Resolution of Dimension 4

M. E. Meehan · J. Torre-Mayo et al.

Linear Algebra and its Applications, 426(2–3): 729–773

Completed the solution of the general complex NIEP for $n = 4$ (following Loewy & London's 1978 solution of the real $n = 4$ and trace-zero $n = 4$ cases), characterizing the exact nonlinear coefficient inequalities beyond JLL bounds needed when a $4 \times 4$ spectrum contains a nonreal conjugate pair.
