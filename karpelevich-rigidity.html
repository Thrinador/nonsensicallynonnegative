The boundary equations use one contact weight. Our polygon begins with a different weight at every edge contact. The missing step is rigidity: unequal neighboring weights would let us move the polygon while keeping it invariant, with one image now strictly inside.

That would contradict what extremality forces. The polygon can change, but its multiplier stays fixed throughout the argument.

Karpelevich uses this strategy twice. A projective variation first eliminates certain return patterns. A simpler slide then forces the surviving weights to agree. We will prove the global projective variation, including its critical derivative-one case, and then prove the weight calculation explicitly, including the zero-weight case that finishes the reduction.

Move a generator and its whole chain

Continue with a nonreal radial extremum $\lambda$ of least realizing order $n>3$, with $0<|\lambda|<1$. The origin is interior to every invariant polygon under consideration. Every image vertex lies on its boundary.

The return arithmetic organized the vertices into chains

$$x_i,\lambda x_i,\ldots,\lambda^{h_i-1}x_i.$$

To move a generating vertex $x_i$ to $x_i'$, move every vertex it generates to $\lambda^j x_i'$. All power relations within the chosen chains remain exact. The remaining checks concern the terminal images at the chain ends.

Figure 1. An exact chain illustration with $\lambda=(1+i)/2$, $z=1$, and $z_s=1+i/5$. Each displacement is the preceding displacement multiplied by $\lambda$. This illustrates propagation along a chain; no polygon-invariance claim is needed for this picture.

A sufficiently small motion keeps the vertices extreme and in the same cyclic order: a finite set of strict convexity inequalities remains strict. To contradict extremality, we must then verify two things:

  1. Every new vertex image still belongs to the new polygon.
  2. At least one belongs to its interior.

An interior image alone is insufficient if some other image has escaped. This is why the directions of motion and the supporting edges matter.

Projective geometry eliminates a return pattern

Write $\varphi$ for the contact-block size and $g=\gcd(n,k)$. In the proper-block case $g<\varphi<n$, Stop 4 found an even-position Euclidean remainder $r$ with

$$0<r<\varphi,\qquad n=p\varphi+qr.$$

Karpelevich’s geometric reduction, Theorem V. Under the extremal hypotheses above, $r=1$. Every Euclidean remainder is a multiple of $g$, so this forces $g=1$. The same part of the paper shows that a full block ($\varphi=n$) has shift $k=1$, which puts it in the forward contact form described below . We prove both conclusions below, including the degenerate closing comparison and all other image constraints.

We now supply the geometric checks behind Theorem V. The multiplier stays fixed; all motions below are motions of the polygon. Write $Tz=\lambda z$ and let

$$E_j=[x_{j-1},x_j],\qquad \xi_j=\alpha_jx_{j-1}+\beta_jx_j\in(x_{j-1},x_j), \quad 1\leq j\leq\varphi.$$

The reduced block consists of genuine edge contacts, so both coefficients are positive. Outside the block the image is the vertex specified by the cyclic shift. We use the orientation with every second-endpoint coefficient positive and $1\leq k<n$. If the initial sector has $k=n$, no alpha coefficient can vanish: that would give $\lambda x_i=x_i$, hence $\lambda=1$. All coefficients are therefore positive in that sector, and conjugation gives the allowed orientation with shift $1$.

For a proper block with $\varphi>g$, the return arithmetic gives

$$n=p\varphi+qr,\qquad \sigma(j)=1+((j+r-1)\bmod\varphi),$$
$$H_j=\begin{cases}p,&j\leq\varphi-r,\\p+q,&j>\varphi-r,\end{cases} \qquad T^{H_j}x_j=\xi_{\sigma(j)},\qquad T^qx_\varphi=x_0.$$

Here $1\leq r<\varphi$, $\gcd(r,\varphi)=g$, and the lists $T^t x_j$, $0\leq t<H_j$, partition all $n$ vertices. Thus $H_j$ includes the last step into an edge contact. For the full block $\varphi=n$, these same identities hold directly with $p=1$, $q=0$, and $r=k$: every chain contains just its starting vertex. The proof will cover that case too.

Lemma 5.1First obtain lines that expose the moving vertices

A supporting line exposes a vertex when its intersection with the polygon is that vertex alone. This strict condition is what makes the projections well defined.

Two simple face facts give the required lines. First, a nonvertex cannot become a vertex under an invertible affine map carrying a polygon into itself. An interior point stays interior. A point inside an edge is a positive combination of its endpoints; if its image were a vertex, both endpoint images would have to equal that vertex, contradicting invertibility. The assertion remains true after further iterations.

Second, under the half-open contact convention, if $T^aE_j$ stays on the boundary, then

$$T^t x_{j-1}=x_{j-1+tk},\qquad0\leq t\leq a.$$

For one step the images of the two endpoints belong to successive half-open edges. Their joining segment can lie in a single boundary edge only if the first image is the common vertex. To iterate, choose a point interior to that image segment. It is interior to the next assigned edge. If its later image is on the boundary, a supporting functional shows that the images of both endpoints of the entire assigned edge lie on that same supporting line. Induction proves the displayed identities.

For $j\geq2$, define

$$L_j=T^{-H_j}\operatorname{aff}(E_{\sigma(j)}).$$

Since $T^{H_j}Q\subseteq Q$, this line supports $Q$ at $x_j$. It cannot contain $E_{j+1}$: the preceding-endpoint fact would make $T^{H_j}x_j$ a vertex, although it is the interior contact $\xi_{\sigma(j)}$. It cannot contain $E_j$ either: that would make $T^{H_j}x_{j-1}$ a vertex, although $x_{j-1}$ has already reached its first nonvertex image at time $H_{j-1}\leq H_j$. Consequently

$$\boxed{L_j\cap Q=\{x_j\}.}$$

This also proves exposure across the change from height $p$ to height $p+q$.

Lemma 5.2A projective-chain lemma, including the critical case Karpelevich §4, pp. 372–373

Take distinct consecutive vertices $X_0,X_1,\ldots,X_{h+1}$ of a convex polygon, in either traversal direction, with $h\geq2$. Assume at least one polygon side lies outside this chain. Let

$$C_i\in(X_{i-1},X_i)\quad(2\leq i\leq h+1),$$

and let $L_i$ expose $X_i$ for $2\leq i\leq h$. Starting on the line $X_0X_1$, project through $C_2$ onto $L_2$, then through $C_i$ onto $L_i$, and finally through $X_{h+1}$ onto the line $K=C_hC_{h+1}$. Call the composite map $\Pi$.

Each projection is a projectivity: its center lies on neither its source nor its target line. Exposure excludes the centers from the $L_i$, and strict convexity excludes the remaining coincidences. Completed lines, with one point at infinity added, allow the same map to be evaluated even when an intermediate ordinary intersection is parallel. We will show

$$\boxed{\Pi(X_1)=C_{h+1},\qquad \Pi(X_0)\in(C_{h+1},C_h).}$$

The first identity follows by following the original vertices through the projections. Here is a coordinate proof of the strict second assertion.

Choose nonnegative affine functions $\eta_0,\eta_1$ on the polygon that vanish only at $X_0,X_{h+1}$ respectively. For each endpoint, add affine functions defining its two incident supporting half-planes. Their sum has precisely the required zero. Then $\eta=\eta_0+\eta_1$ is strictly positive on the whole polygon. Choose an affine function $\psi$ independent of $\eta_0,\eta$, and make the projective change

$$x\longmapsto\left(\frac{\eta_0(x)}{\eta(x)},\frac{\psi(x)}{\eta(x)}\right).$$

Its denominator is positive, so it preserves segments and their interiors. For example, the image of $(1-s)x+sy$ is the convex combination of the two transformed endpoints with weights proportional to $(1-s)\eta(x)$ and $s\eta(y)$.

The first coordinate now has unique minimum zero at $X_0$ and unique maximum one at $X_{h+1}$. The selected boundary chain is a graph; reflect the second coordinate if necessary to make it the lower graph. Write

$$X_i=(t_i,f_i),\qquad t_0<\cdots<t_{h+1},\qquad d_i=\frac{f_i-f_{i-1}}{t_i-t_{i-1}}.$$

Convexity gives $d_1<\cdots<d_{h+1}$. If $s_i$ is the slope of $L_i$, exposure gives

$$d_i<s_i<d_{i+1}.$$

Put $s_1=d_1$, $a_i=t_i-t_{i-1}>0$, and write

$$C_i=X_i-\gamma_i a_i(1,d_i),\qquad0<\gamma_i<1.$$

Starting at $Z_1=X_0$, seek the successive projected points as

$$Z_i=X_i-r_i(1,s_i),\qquad r_1=a_1.$$

Collinearity of $Z_{i-1},C_i,Z_i$ is the single determinant equation

$$\det\left((1-\gamma_i)a_i(1,d_i)+r_{i-1}(1,s_{i-1}),\ \gamma_i a_i(1,d_i)-r_i(1,s_i)\right)=0.$$

Writing $z_i=1/r_i$ turns it into

$$\boxed{z_i= \frac{(1-\gamma_i)(s_i-d_i)}{\gamma_i(d_i-s_{i-1})}z_{i-1} +\frac{s_i-s_{i-1}}{\gamma_i a_i(d_i-s_{i-1})}.}$$

Both coefficients are strictly positive. Starting from $z_1=1/a_1$, we obtain finite positive values and

$$0<r_i<\gamma_i a_i\frac{d_i-s_{i-1}}{s_i-s_{i-1}}<\gamma_i a_i.$$

For the final projection abbreviate

$$r=r_h,\quad b=\gamma_h a_h,\quad A=a_{h+1},\quad d=d_h,\quad s=s_h,\quad D=d_{h+1}.$$

The affine function $G(W)=\det(X_{h+1}-Z_h,W-Z_h)$ vanishes on the final projection line. Direct substitution gives

$$\begin{aligned} G(C_h)&=A(b-r)(D-s)+b(A+r)(s-d)>0,\\ G(C_{h+1})&=-\gamma_{h+1}Ar(D-s)<0. \end{aligned}$$

Thus that line crosses the open segment between the two contacts. Returning to the original coordinates preserves this strict segment inclusion. This proves the boxed projective-chain assertion, without relying on a drawing or assuming intermediate intersections are finite in the original chart.

Now let $S$ be a projectivity sending $X_1$ to $C_{h+1}$ and $X_0$ to $C_h$. Parametrize the latter line by

$$Y(t)=(1-t)C_{h+1}+tC_h,\qquad X_1(t)=S^{-1}Y(t).$$

Propagate this point through the same projections to get $X_i(t)$. Near zero all points are finite and depend smoothly on $t$. Let $u(t)$ be the coordinate where the moving final line $X_h(t)X_{h+1}$ meets $K$. The composite is projective, so

$$u(t)=\frac{at}{1+ct},\qquad u(0)=0,\qquad0<u(1)<1.$$

Increasing the coordinate from $C_{h+1}$ toward $C_h$ enters the inward side of the original final edge. Therefore the signed inward distance of $Y(t)$ from the moving edge has the sign of $t-u(t)$ for small $t$. Exactly,

$$t-u(t)=\frac{(1-a)t+ct^2}{1+ct}.$$

If $a\ne1$, choose the sign of a sufficiently small $t$ to make the linear term positive. If $a=1$, the proved inequality $0<u(1)<1$ gives $c>0$, so either sufficiently small nonzero sign works. In both cases the closing image lies strictly on the inward side. This proves the local variation even when its first derivative vanishes.

Choose the moving chain in both placements

Suppose, seeking a contradiction, that $r>1$. There are two possible placements of the moving block. The second is handled by traversing the polygon in the opposite direction:

Condition Consecutive vertices $X_i$, $0\leq i\leq h+1$ Moving-chain length $h$ Power $S=T^a$
$2r\leq\varphi+1$ $X_i=x_i$ $r$ $a=p$
$2r>\varphi+1$ $X_i=x_{\varphi-i}$ $\varphi-r+1$ $a=p+q$

In both rows,

$$2\leq h\leq(\varphi+1)/2.$$

The chain uses $h+1$ polygon sides. If $\varphi<n$, then $h+1\leq\varphi<n$. If $\varphi=n\geq4$, then $h+1\leq(n+3)/2<n$. Thus the chain is proper, and its endpoints are distinct. This is exactly where the order-three exception is excluded.

Keep $X_0,X_{h+1}$ fixed and move $X_1,\ldots,X_h$. For $i\geq2$, use the exposing line already proved for the corresponding base. Take $C_i$ to be the original contact on the side $[X_{i-1},X_i]$. The return identities give

$$T^aX_0=C_h,\qquad T^aX_1=C_{h+1}.$$

For the forward row these contacts are $\xi_r,\xi_{r+1}$; for the reverse row they are $\xi_r,\xi_{r-1}$. Hence the restriction of $T^a$ supplies exactly the map $S$ needed in the projective-chain lemma.

Theorem 5.3Check every image, not just the closing one Karpelevich §4, Thm. V

Let $M$ be the moving base indices, let $J$ be the indices of the internal chain sides, and let $k_*$ be the final side index. Write $\varepsilon=1$ in the forward row and $\varepsilon=-1$ in the reverse row. Modulo $\varphi$,

$$\sigma(M)=\{k_*+\varepsilon t:0\leq t<h\},\qquad J=\{k_*-\varepsilon t:1\leq t<h\}.$$

These sets are disjoint. An intersection would require $\varphi$ to divide a positive integer at most $2h-2$, whereas $2h-2<\varphi$.

Extend the base motions uniquely along the vertex chains:

$$\widehat{T^t x_j}=T^t\widehat x_j,\qquad0\leq t<H_j.$$

The chain partition makes this a consistent prescription for all vertices. The following list exhausts their images:

Image type Why it stays in the moved polygon
Inside a power chain It is exactly the next moved vertex.
Return to an internal moving side Its source base is fixed because $J\cap\sigma(M)=\varnothing$. Projection through $C_i$ keeps the contact on that side.
Return from a moving base other than $X_1$ Its target line is fixed. The base stays on the appropriate preimage line $L_j$, so its terminal image stays on that target line.
Other returns Both the base and the containing line are fixed; the image is unchanged.
Return from $X_1$ The projective-chain lemma puts it strictly inward of the final moving side.

The initial side line is fixed even though its endpoint $X_1$ moves along it. Also $x_0=T^q x_\varphi$ stays fixed because the base $x_\varphi$ stays fixed. These observations cover the end identifications in the two rows.

All preserved terminal images start strictly between their side endpoints. Smallness keeps them there. The exceptional image also starts strictly inside its side, so it remains strictly inside the half-planes of all the other sides. Choose the motion small enough to preserve these inequalities and the strict convexity of the finite vertex list. The lemma makes the exceptional image strictly inward of its remaining side as well.

We have therefore constructed an invariant convex $n$-gon with an interior image of a vertex. Stop 2 forbids it. The assumption $r>1$ is impossible:

$$\boxed{r=1,\qquad g=\gcd(r,\varphi)=1.}$$

For the full block, $r=k$, so the chosen orientation has $k=1$. The argument proves both reductions used below.

The construction follows Karpelevich’s Theorem V. The exposing-line and positive-coordinate proof above is a detailed version of the projective argument; compare Verbeken–Ginis (2026), Lemmas 3.3–3.4 and Theorem 3.5. Every ingredient used here is proved above or in Stops 2–4; no boundary-arc theorem is assumed.

An exact local model of the closing inequality

The return-map calculation has two cases. Exactly,

$$\boxed{s-u(s)=\frac{s\bigl((1-a)+cs\bigr)}{1+cs}.}$$

If $a\ne1$, choose a sufficiently small $s$ with the sign of $1-a$. The numerator and denominator then give $s-u(s)>0$.

If $a=1$, the first-order difference vanishes. The projective-chain lemma above proves $0<u(1)<1$ in the relevant nondegenerate configuration. Since $u(1)=1/(1+c)$, this forces $c>0$. Therefore

$$s-u(s)=\frac{cs^2}{1+cs}>0$$

for every sufficiently small nonzero $s$.

Why the low-order exception is real

Let $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/3}$ and take the regular triangle $Q=\operatorname{conv}\{1,\zeta,\zeta^2\}$. The multiplier $\lambda=-1/2-i/2$ lies strictly inside the edge $[\zeta,\zeta^2]$. Rotational symmetry puts every image vertex inside its opposite edge.

Thus $n=3$, $k=2$, and $\varphi=3$: the full block has selected remainder $r=k=2$. This multiplier is radially extremal by the three-state trace bound $1+2\operatorname{Re}\lambda\geq0$, and two-state stochastic spectra are real. The conclusion that the selected remainder is one cannot be extended to this order.

Put the surviving relations in a usable form

After $r=1$, let $m$ be the preceding odd Euclidean remainder. The contact block has $\varphi\leq m$. Apply the return arithmetic to the larger marked window $1,\ldots,m$, and write $\ell$ and $\tau$ for that window’s two return times (the $p$ and $q$ of Stop 4). Stop 7 shows that $\tau<\ell$, so $m=\lfloor n/\ell\rfloor$.

Some added marks may be vertex contacts. Encode those by $\alpha_j=0$, $\beta_j=1$. The resulting relations are

$$\begin{aligned} \lambda^\ell x_i&=\alpha_{i+1}x_i+\beta_{i+1}x_{i+1}, &&0\leq i<m,\\ \lambda^\tau x_m&=x_0, &&n=\ell m+\tau, \end{aligned}$$

where $\alpha_j\geq0$, $\beta_j>0$, and $\alpha_j+\beta_j=1$. The full-block case has $\ell=1$, $\tau=0$, $m=n$, with $x_m=x_0$.

We have not assumed that the marked window consists entirely of edge contacts. Showing that its weights agree will force every $\alpha_j$ to be positive, because at least one genuine edge contact is present. It will also show $\varphi=m$.

The other branch, $\varphi=g$, has $m=g$ equal-length chains from the outset:

$$\lambda^\ell x_i=\alpha_i x_{i-1}+\beta_i x_i, \quad1\leq i\leq g,\qquad \lambda^\sigma x_g=x_0,\qquad\ell=n/g.$$

Here all contact weights are positive, and $\sigma$ is the closing shift. Notice the different edge: the first branch uses the generator and its successor; the second uses its predecessor and the generator.

Theorem 5.4Unequal weights create an interior image Karpelevich §4, Thm. VI

Consider neighboring coefficients $a=\alpha_i$ and $b=\alpha_{i+1}$ in the forward branch. For this local calculation write

$$A=x_{i-1},\qquad B=x_i,\qquad C=x_{i+1},\qquad T=\lambda^\ell.$$

The two contacts are

$$\xi=TA=aA+(1-a)B,\qquad \eta=TB=bB+(1-b)C.$$

Move the middle generator along the preceding edge line:

$$\boxed{B_s=B+s(B-A).}$$

Move its whole generated chain with it; keep the other chains fixed. The first contact remains on the same line. Its exact new expression is

$$\xi=\frac{a+s}{1+s}A+\frac{1-a}{1+s}B_s.$$

For sufficiently small allowed $s$, both coefficients are nonnegative. So this contact remains inside the new edge.

The moving chain’s terminal image is

$$\eta_s=TB_s=\eta+s(\eta-\xi).$$

Express it in the triangle with vertices $A,B_s,C$:

$$\eta_s=w_AA+w_BB_s+w_CC,$$

where

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned} w_A&=\frac{s\bigl((b-a)-s(1-b)\bigr)}{1+s},\\ w_B&=b-\frac{s(1-a)}{1+s},\\ w_C&=(1+s)(1-b). \end{aligned}}$$

The coefficients sum to one. If $a\ne b$, choose a sufficiently small $s$ with the sign of $b-a$. Then all three coefficients are strictly positive. Thus $\eta_s$ lies strictly inside that triangle, and hence strictly inside the new polygon.

Only these two contacts need new checks. Every other terminal contact is unchanged; every image within a chain follows the propagated vertices. Smallness preserves the polygon’s convexity. We have therefore retained invariance and created an interior image of a vertex—a contradiction.

It follows that $\alpha_i=\alpha_{i+1}$. Apply this for $i=1,\ldots,m-1$:

$$\boxed{\alpha_1=\cdots=\alpha_m=\alpha, \qquad\beta_1=\cdots=\beta_m=1-\alpha.}$$

At equal weights, this attempted slide has $w_A=-(1-a)s^2/(1+s)<0$ for nonzero small $s$. The same motion now points outside the supporting edge.

The coefficient calculation, including a possible zero weight

Substitute

$$B=\frac{B_s+sA}{1+s}$$

into

$$\eta_s=(1+s)\bigl(bB+(1-b)C\bigr)-s\bigl(aA+(1-a)B\bigr).$$

Collect the coefficients of $A,B_s,C$. This gives the boxed formulas and their sum one.

Since $a,b<1$, the coefficient $w_C$ is positive for small $s$. The chosen sign makes $w_A>0$. If $b>0$, then $w_B>0$ by continuity. If $b=0$, then $a>0$ and the chosen $s$ is negative, so $w_B=-s(1-a)/(1+s)>0$ as well.

The fixed contact’s coefficient $(a+s)/(1+s)$ also stays positive: use smallness if $a>0$; if $a=0$, then $b>0$ and the chosen sign is positive. Thus the proof includes unequal pairs in which one alpha coefficient is zero.

This rules out zero coefficients in the enlarged window once any alpha is positive. Consequently every mark is a genuine edge contact, and $\varphi=m$.

Theorem 5.5The gcd branch uses the opposite slide Karpelevich §4, Thm. VII

For $\varphi=g$, put $T=\lambda^\ell$ and take three neighboring generators $A,B,C$. The contact equations now read

$$TB=aA+(1-a)B,\qquad TC=bB+(1-b)C.$$

Slide toward the opposite edge line:

$$B_s=B+s(B-C).$$

The fixed image $TC$ remains on the new edge $[B_s,C]$, since

$$TC=\frac{b}{1+s}B_s+\frac{1-b+s}{1+s}C.$$

The moved image has coefficients

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned} TB_s&=v_AA+v_BB_s+v_CC,\\ v_A&=(1+s)a,\\ v_B&=(1-a)-\frac{sb}{1+s},\\ v_C&=\frac{s\bigl((b-a)-sa\bigr)}{1+s}. \end{aligned}}$$

Again they sum to one. All contact weights in this branch are positive. If $a\ne b$, a sufficiently small $s$ with the sign of $b-a$ makes all three coefficients positive. This gives the same contradiction, with the same chain and convexity checks.

Compare consecutive pairs for $i=1,\ldots,g-1$, leaving the last generating chain and its closing relation fixed. All $g$ weights agree. If $g=1$, there is only one weight and nothing to compare.

What is rigid now?

The geometric step has supplied two conclusions that arithmetic alone could not give:

Branch Surviving structure Common weights
$\varphi>g$ $g=1$, selected remainder $1$, $\varphi=m$ chains of length $\ell$ $\alpha_j=\alpha$, $\beta_j=\beta$
$\varphi=g$ $m=g$ chains of length $\ell=n/g$ $\alpha_j=\alpha$, $\beta_j=\beta$

In both cases $\alpha,\beta>0$ and $\alpha+\beta=1$. The terminal equations now have one shared parameter. Eliminating the generating coordinates gives

$$\lambda^\tau(\lambda^\ell-t)^m=(1-t)^m,\qquad t=\alpha,$$

in the forward case, and

$$(\lambda^\ell-t)^m=(1-t)^m\lambda^\sigma,\qquad t=\beta,$$

in the gcd case. In both branches $t$ is the weight on the chain’s own generator, the local return. That is $\alpha$ in the forward branch and $\beta$ in the gcd branch, because the two branches use opposite edges. This is the convention for $t$ throughout the series.

The boundary-equations post carries out those eliminations. The work here explains why the repeated factors have the same coefficient: every unequal neighboring pair would admit an invariant variation that extremality forbids.
